Step 5: Newton’s second law

📖 Definition (Newton’s second law): The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the resultant force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass. The acceleration is in the same direction as the resultant force.

In symbols (resultant force = mass × acceleration):

F = m × aa = F ÷ mm = F ÷ a

F is the resultant force (N), m the mass (the amount of matter in the object, in kg), a the acceleration (m/s²). 1 newton is the force that gives a mass of 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s².

Step 6: Same push, different mass

Newton’s second law says a = F ÷ m. Let’s test it. All three blocks get the same push (8 N) but have different masses. Press Race!. If the mass doubles, the acceleration halves.

💡 Same force: bigger mass → smaller acceleration. This matches a = F ÷ m.

Step 7: Same mass, different push

Now test the law the other way round. All three blocks have the same mass (2 kg) but different pushes. Press Race!. If the push doubles, the acceleration doubles.

💡 Same mass: bigger force → bigger acceleration. This matches a = F ÷ m.

Examples: from easy to harder

Example 1: find the acceleration

A 1200 kg car has a resultant force of 3000 N.

  1. Given: m = 1200 kg, F = 3000 N, a = ?
  2. Formula: a = F ÷ m
  3. Substitute: a = 3000 ÷ 1200
  4. Answer: a = 2.5 m/s²

Common mistakes

• A force is not needed to keep a constant speed.
• Use the resultant force in F = m × a.
• Mass is in kg and force is in N.
• Always write the unit.
