01 · THE IDEASame size, different “heaviness”

In everyday talk, lead is “heavier” than wood. Scientists mean that a certain volume of lead has more mass than the same volume of wood. We compare using density: the mass per unit volume of a substance. Pick two materials and one volume, and the balance shows which has more mass.

02 · DEFINITIONDensity = mass ÷ volume

ρ = m ÷ Vm = V × ρV = m ÷ ρ

ρ (rho) is density, m is mass, V is volume. Lead’s density is 11 g/cm³: 1 cm³ of lead has a mass of 11 g, and 5 cm³ has 55 g. Engineers use this to find the weight of a structure from its plans and plan strong foundations. Below, the dots show how much matter is packed into 1 cm³.

03 · UNITSg/cm³ and kg/m³

The SI unit of density is kg/m³. Since 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ and 1 kg = 1000 g, to convert g/cm³ → kg/m³ multiply by 10³. Water: 1.0 g/cm³ = 1.0 × 10³ kg/m³.

05 · CALCULATIONSSolve for any unknown

Find ρ g/cm³ m g V cm³

Worked example (book): density of copper = 9 g/cm³. Find (a) the mass of 5 cm³ and (b) the volume of 63 g.
Show solution(a) m = V × ρ = 5 × 9 = 45 g
(b) V = m ÷ ρ = 63 ÷ 9 = 7 cm³
Real example: a room 10 m × 5.0 m × 2.0 m holds 100 m³ of air (ρ = 1.3 kg/m³).
Show solutionm = V × ρ = 100 × 1.3 = 130 kg: as heavy as two adults!

06 · MEASURING DENSITYFour experiments

Regular solid (block): find mass on a balance; find volume from ruler measurements (l × b × h). Then ρ = m ÷ V.

Irregular solid (pebble, glass stopper): mass on a balance; volume by displacement: V = (2nd reading) − (1st reading) of a measuring cylinder. Drop the solid in:

Solid

Liquid: (1) weigh an empty beaker. (2) Pour in a known volume from a burette or measuring cylinder. (3) Weigh beaker + liquid. (4) Subtract to get the liquid’s mass, then ρ = m ÷ V.

Air: (1) weigh a 500 cm³ flask full of air. (2) Pump the air out with a vacuum pump and weigh again; the difference is the air’s mass. (3) Fill the flask with water and pour it into a measuring cylinder to find the volume. ρ = m ÷ V.

07 · FLOATING & SINKINGAn object sinks in a liquid of lower density than its own

Otherwise it floats, partly or wholly submerged. Glass (2.5 g/cm³) sinks in water but floats in mercury. An iron nail sinks but an iron ship floats: its average density (hollow hull full of air) is less than water’s. Try the Dead Sea too!

08 · DENSITY TOWERLayers of liquids

Paraffin (0.80), water (1.0) and mercury (13.6) stack in layers because they don’t mix and the densest sits at the bottom. Drop objects in: each stops at the layer that is denser than it.

10 · INTERVIEW PREPExplain it in plain words

What is density?Mass per unit volume: ρ = m ÷ V. It tells you how much matter is packed into a given space, e.g. 1 cm³ of lead has 11 g, 1 cm³ of water only 1 g.
Why does ice float on water?Ice has density 0.92 g/cm³, lower than water’s 1.0 g/cm³, so it floats with most of it submerged.
Why does an iron nail sink but a steel ship float?Compare average density. A nail is solid iron (7.9 g/cm³). A ship’s hollow hull is mostly air, so total mass ÷ total volume is below water’s density.
How do you find the density of a pebble?Weigh it (mass). Put it in a measuring cylinder of water and find the volume from the rise in water level (or collect the overflow from a displacement can). Then ρ = m ÷ V.
Convert 2.7 g/cm³ to kg/m³Multiply by 10³: 2.7 × 10³ = 2700 kg/m³ (aluminium).
Is “heavier” the same as “denser”?No. A huge block of wood can be heavier than a small lead ball, but lead is denser: more mass in every cm³.
